# Tidal Almanac

Tidal Almanac treats time as a navigable body of water. Space is measured in calm intervals rather than boxes: two unequal folios, ruled like an old observatory ledger, separated by a narrow channel of light. Geometry is exact and patient, built from hairline grids, concentric arcs, coordinate ticks, and restrained corner marks. Nothing is decorative without a spatial purpose; every line helps the eye locate a date, a duration, or a recurring intention.

Color behaves like depth. Abyssal blue establishes quiet authority, oxidized cyan marks living structure, and pale gold is reserved for the present moment and moments of completion. Small mineral accents may differentiate kinds of work, but they never compete with the main tide. Surfaces should feel like smoked glass over drafting paper, with translucent layers that reveal structure without becoming noisy.

Rhythm comes from patient repetition. Seven vertical measures recall daily tides; repeated labels, fine rules, and systematic markers create the confidence of a scientific instrument. One mark may glow, but only because the surrounding field is disciplined enough to let it speak. Hierarchy is achieved through spacing, weight, and luminance rather than oversized type or ornamental excess.

Typography is clinical at small scale and ceremonial only at the point of action. Labels read as engraved captions; headings feel cut by hand into the frame; numerals align like readings from a precise instrument. Text remains sparse and essential. The composition should be understood spatially before it is read.

The final work must appear meticulously crafted, as though it passed through countless rounds of alignment, contrast testing, and restraint. It should feel painstakingly calibrated by someone at the absolute top of their field: quiet, luxurious, exact, and unmistakably intentional. The aim is not to add more objects, but to make every existing object inevitable.
